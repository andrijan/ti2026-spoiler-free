How this stays spoiler-free

Every series shows all of its slots — three for a Bo3, five for a Bo5 — whether or not the game was actually played. The number of links can never tell you that a series ended 2–0. If you open a slot for a game that was never played, the site says so at that moment, and not before.

Rounds start collapsed so a later matchup can't spoil an earlier result just by sitting on screen. Open one when you're ready for it.

Reveal modes

  • Progressive (default) — a slot opens up once you've ticked the game before it. You always know what you're about to watch, never what comes after.
  • Strict — nothing at all until you press the ? on a specific game. The series rating stays hidden too.
  • Open — everything visible. Use this once you've caught up.

What the ratings mean

Ratings are shown per series only — enough to tell you a good game is in there somewhere, never which one it is. There's no public site that rates individual pro Dota games, so this one scores them from the replay data itself (via OpenDota). Four signals, ranked against every other game at this TI:

  • Comeback — the biggest gold deficit the eventual winner climbed out of
  • Lead swings — how often the gold lead genuinely changed hands
  • Closeness — how tight the gold margin stayed through the back half
  • Kill pace — kills per minute

A series is flagged ★★ must-watch inside if one of its games lands in the top 15% at this TI, or ★ worth watching for the next 25%. A rating never reveals who won.

Game length is never shown. A revealed slot is marked ⏱ short if it ran under 35 minutes, and carries no mark otherwise — so you can skip the quick ones without knowing how long the rest are.
